**Priority order (by proven conversion):**

**One hard rule before the templates:** we never send unsolicited bulk email to private
individuals. It's illegal in the UK/EU (GDPR + PECR — fines are real), and it doesn't convert
anyway. "Cold email" in this playbook means **outreach to creators, newsletters, and
businesses** (legitimate interest, one-to-one, easy opt-out) — never scraping "people who
like protein recipes" and mass-mailing them. The compliant way to email *individuals* is to
earn their address: free 7-day plan → Kit list → email them forever. That machine is built.

## 2. Engagement comments — "borrowing" audiences politely

**The strategy:** every day, spend 15 minutes as @the70gprotein / @the70g_protein leaving
genuinely useful comments on OTHER accounts' high-protein/fitness-food content. People who
read comments on a protein-recipe video are exactly your audience; a smart chef comment makes
30–50 of them tap your profile. It's the cheapest targeted traffic that exists.

**Where:** TikTok + Instagram search: `high protein recipes`, `high protein meals`,
`protein dessert`, `what I eat in a day protein`, `high protein meal prep`, `75 hard diet`,
`cutting meals`. Sort by recent; pick posts <24h old with traction (they're still being pushed).

**The 6 rules:** be useful or funny, never salesy · NEVER paste a link · never say "check my
page" (the profile visit happens on its own) · comment as the chef, with a real tip · 5–10/day
max, spread out (bursts look like a bot) · reply if they reply.

**Comment templates (adapt, don't paste identically twice):**

On a chicken/protein recipe video:
> Chef tip if you want this even juicier — salt the chicken 20 min before, not during. Night and day difference. Lovely recipe 👏

On a dry-looking high-protein meal:
> The protein's there but I'd fight for a sauce 😄 quick one: Greek yogurt + lemon + the pan juices. Zero effort, saves the whole plate.

On a protein dessert:
> As a chef I love seeing protein desserts that don't taste like the tub. Cocoa + a pinch of salt is doing the heavy lifting here and I respect it.

On a "struggling to hit my protein" post:
> The trick that changed it for my clients: build the plate around 2 protein sources, not 1. Chicken + a yogurt-based sauce gets you 15g further without eating more.

On a "what I eat in a day":
> Solid day of eating. If breakfast ever gets boring — eggs love smoked fish, 5-min upgrade, huge protein bump 🐟

On a vegetarian protein post:
> Halloumi, paneer and eggs are so underrated for this — 27 of my 100 recipes are veggie and they were honestly the most fun to build. Nice work with this one.

On anything genuinely good:
> This is the kind of high-protein cooking that actually looks like food. Saved 👌

## 3. Creator & newsletter outreach — the legitimate "cold" channel

Full strategy in `IDEAS.md` (gift-first, 50% Gumroad affiliate). **Unlocked when you have
3+ reviews** — reviews are the proof creators check. These are the templates for that moment.

**Where to find them:**
- **TikTok/IG:** search the hashtags above; shortlist accounts with 5–50k followers, UK-lean,
  real engagement (comments, not just likes). The "1 mutual" suggestions IG shows you count too.
- **YouTube:** search "high protein recipes", filter channels 10–100k subs — YouTubers convert
  best (clickable descriptions).
- **Newsletters:** fitness/food newsletters on Substack (search "high protein", "nutrition",
  "fitness") and beehiiv trending pages. Small paid-subscriber newsletters LOVE good affiliate
  offers.
- Keep a 3-column list in your notes: handle · platform · why them (Claude can build this list
  on request).

**Template A — the gift DM (first contact, no ask):**
> Hi [name] — I'm Bengisu, a professional chef (ex head chef). I spent this year building a
> cookbook where every recipe hits ~70g of protein without tasting like a chore — and your
> content is exactly the audience I made it for. No ask here: I'd just love to send you the
> PDF as a gift. If you ever cook something from it, tell me which one you'd pick first.

**Template B — the affiliate invite (ONLY after they respond warmly):**
> So glad you liked it. If you'd ever want to share it with your audience, I set up an
> affiliate link that pays you 50% of every sale (£9.50 per book — Gumroad tracks and pays
> automatically, zero admin). Absolutely no pressure — and if you do post, it legally needs
> an #ad tag in the UK. Your words, your way; I'd never send a script.

**Template C — the newsletter pitch (email, short):**
> Subject: 50% affiliate — chef-built high-protein cookbook (fits your readers)
>
> Hi [name] — I read [newsletter] and your piece on [specific recent piece]. I'm a UK chef
> (ex sous/head chef) and I've built a 118-page PDF cookbook: 100 recipes, each ~70g protein
> and under 20g net carbs, 27 vegetarian. £19 on Gumroad, [X] rating so far.
> Happy to send you the full book free to review, and if it fits, my affiliate link pays 50%
> (£9.50/sale, Gumroad pays out automatically). One reply and I'll set both up.
> — Bengisu · the70gprotein.com

**Follow-up rule:** one polite nudge after 5–7 days, then stop. Track: sent / replied / gifted /
affiliate live.
